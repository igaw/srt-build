Dashboards and the normal range
===============================

``round export`` sends every finished job of a round to VictoriaMetrics;
``jobs dashboard DIR --lava-url URL`` writes the Grafana dashboards that
show it (generated by ``srt_build/grafana.py``; edit that and rerun, not
the JSON). This page explains what they show and how the green band, the
*normal range*, is computed: so the numbers can be explained to whoever
asks.

The dashboards
--------------

Four dashboards, linked to each other at the top right:

RT tests
    The overview: one row per board, in it one trend per test. The x
    axis is the release round (``10``, ``11``, ...), the y axis the max
    latency of that round (largest of its jobs and threads), on a log
    scale; a line per flavor and workload. The green band and red points
    are explained below. A click on a panel's title opens it in detail.

RT test detail
    One board, flavor and test: the max latency with its normal range,
    the max and average latency per thread, percentiles from the
    histograms, one round in depth (histogram, threads), the rounds,
    jobs, problems and kernels. The *round kind* selector (release,
    test, All) decides which rounds the trends show; a test round
    (``t5``) is drawn at its number (5), with its own lines marked
    "(test)".

RT rounds compared
    Two rounds side by side, release or test, chosen at its top (round
    A, round B): a bar per board, test and workload.

RT problems
    The jobs with problems (failed health, failed test cases or uploads,
    definitions without results, kernel splats on the console, a max
    latency above 1 ms) and a few numbers over all jobs chosen.

Release and test rounds
-----------------------

Release rounds (numbers) are the reference runs: every board, flavor and
test, as ``config.yml`` rounds says, also when repeated with ``round
repeat``. Test rounds (``t1``, ``t2``, ...) are one-off experiments: a
single ``lava <board>`` command, other settings. Only release rounds
make the normal range; test rounds are judged against it.

The normal range
----------------

The question the band answers: *is this round's max latency still in
line with the rounds before it?* Nobody has to know the right limit for
each board and test; the earlier rounds give it.

For each panel, from the release rounds it shows (the dashboard's time
range and the selectors: board, flavor, test, kernel, workload):

1. ``x`` is the max latency of each release round (in the overview, the
   largest of the lines shown).
2. ``median`` is the median of the ``x``.
3. ``MAD``, the *median absolute deviation*, is the median of the
   distances ``|x - median|``.
4. ``sigma = 1.4826 x MAD`` estimates the standard deviation from the
   MAD (for normally distributed data the two agree; 1.4826 is the
   constant that makes them).
5. The band's half width is ``3 x sigma``, but at least 40 % of the
   median and at least 20 us.
6. The band is ``median - half`` to ``median + half`` (the lower edge at
   least 1 us, the axis is logarithmic).

A round above the band is drawn once more as a red point: worth a look.
A round below the band is not marked: a lower max latency is better,
not a problem.

In short::

    sigma = 1.4826 * median(|x - median(x)|)
    half  = max(3 * sigma, 0.4 * median(x), 20 us)
    band  = median(x) +- half

VictoriaMetrics computes it on every view (``quantile(0.5, ...)``,
``abs``, ``clamp_min``); nothing about it is stored or exported. A new
round changes the band the moment it is exported.

Why median and MAD
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

The obvious band, mean +- 2 or 3 standard deviations, fails on exactly
the rounds it should catch: a single wild round raises the mean and the
standard deviation so much that it lands inside its own band
(*masking*). Round 11 shows it: on rpi3-64 its stress-ng-scheduler max
was 185 ms against about 1 ms in every other round; the mean +- 2
standard deviations band reached from 0 to 185 ms, everything was
"normal". It cannot be otherwise: among ``n`` values a single one lies
at most ``(n - 1) / sqrt(n)`` standard deviations from their mean, 1.79
for five rounds, so a band of 2 standard deviations can never flag it. The median and the MAD ignore a single outlier (they only
move when half the values do): the band is 324 us to 1.66 ms, round 11
far above it.

This is Hampel's identifier, a standard robust method for outliers. The
classical tests are a poor fit here:

- Student's t-test compares the means of two groups ("is rt24 slower
  than rt23?"); it finds no outliers.
- Grubbs' test and Chauvenet's criterion assume normally distributed
  data and are themselves fooled by the outlier's mean and standard
  deviation. Maximum latencies are not normally distributed: they have
  a long tail towards high values.
- Dixon's Q test is made for 3 to 10 values and a single outlier: it
  would stop working as rounds accumulate.

Why 3 and the minimum width
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

Three standard deviations is the usual limit of a control chart: for
normal data 99.7 % of the values lie within it, so a point outside is
rarely chance.

The minimum width (40 % of the median, 20 us) is ``doc/TUNING.md``'s
rule of thumb: only a change beyond about 40 % and 20 us is worth a
look. It is needed while there are few rounds:

- With four or five rounds the MAD itself is unsteady and often small:
  without the minimum, the first scan of the overview had 33 red points
  in 76 panels, most of them a few percent above a band too narrow.
  With it: 15, twelve of them round 11.
- Values alike to the microsecond have a MAD of 0: c2d's cyclictest was
  11, 11, 12, 12, 12 us, the band 12 to 12 us, and 11 us red.

As rounds accumulate the MAD gets steadier, and the minimum can shrink
or go (``BAND_MIN``, ``BAND_MIN_US`` and ``BAND_K`` in
``srt_build/grafana.py``).

Reading the band
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

- A red point: this round's max latency is far above the others. Look
  at the job (the detail dashboard's Jobs table links to LAVA).
- A wide band: the test scatters a lot by itself (rpi3-32
  stress-ng-scheduler: 0.9 to 50 ms over the rounds). The band says so
  honestly; such a test cannot detect small regressions.
- Normal but high: the band compares with the history, not with what a
  real-time kernel should reach. rpi3-64 stress-ng-io has been 9 to 20
  ms in nearly every round, so 10.9 ms is inside its band, while ``round
  report`` flags anything above 1 ms. Both are right; such a test needs
  a closer look of its own (here: rtla's user-space threads are not
  locked in memory, see ``doc/TUNING.md``).

Limits
~~~~~~

- Few rounds, rough band: with five release rounds the median and MAD
  are estimates on very little data. More rounds of the same kernel
  (``round repeat``, see :doc:`rounds`) make the band reliable; ten or
  more is a sensible goal.
- The band mixes what is selected: with kernel "All" it spans every
  kernel's rounds, with flavor "All" (overview) the largest of the
  flavors. Choose one kernel to see the spread on that kernel alone.
- Rounds of another setup (round 11: the stress-ng set before the
  stressors that ran the boards out of memory were removed) are part of
  the reference. The median keeps them from widening the band, and they
  show as red points; they are not removed from the data.
- A test round is drawn at its number (``t5`` at 5), next to the release
  rounds but not between them; its x position means nothing beyond the
  number.

Gathering more data
-------------------

The band improves with every release round of the same kernel. Repeat a
round on the installed kernels, without rebuilding, in the kernel tree:

.. code-block:: console

  $ srt-build round repeat 15 --yes    # round 15's jobs again, as the next round
  $ srt-build round wait
  $ srt-build round report
  $ srt-build round export             # into VictoriaMetrics, the band follows
